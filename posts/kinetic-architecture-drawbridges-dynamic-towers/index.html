Embracing Kinetic Architecture: From Drawbridges to Dynamic Towers

October 18, 2023 Andres Silva

The roots of kinetic architecture stretch back through the annals of history. Even in the Middle Ages, we find primitive yet compelling examples like drawbridges, enduring testaments to the appeal of dynamic design. However, it wasn’t until the 20th century that architects truly delved into the potential of kinetic architecture.

The artistic movement of Futurism, which emerged in Italy, championed progress and movement as its core tenets, sparking a renewed interest in dynamic structures. This era saw the emergence of influential works like Chernikhov’s “101 Architectural Fantasies” (1933), featuring ambitious plans for movable buildings.

Three Pillars of Kinetic Architecture

Kinetic architecture is a versatile concept, applicable to both statues and entire buildings. Three crucial facets make kinetic architecture a compelling concept.

Incorporation of a Specific Function: The hallmark of kinetic architecture is its ability to imbue structures with multifunctionality. Drawbridges elevate their central sections to accommodate the passage of ships. Stadiums with retractable roofs provide shelter from inclement weather.

Elevating Aesthetic Appeal: Kinetic architecture possesses the transformative power to reshape a building’s visual identity. Moving parts allow for alterations in a building’s appearance — shifting or rotating elements to new configurations. This dynamic transformation can also serve a practical purpose, shielding interiors from excessive sunlight or cold temperatures.

Harnessing Environmental Conditions: Building facades can incorporate hinge or fold effects coupled with solar technologies to generate energy for self-sustained power. The World Trade Center in Bahrain, featuring three central wind turbines, produces up to 15% of the building’s energy requirements from the building’s own motion.

Parametric Geometry in Practice

A simplified parametric model allows the manipulation of geometry in a kinetic manner. In this particular case, points represent sun rays that hit the skin of the building elements and activate to either allow more light in or block it — depending on the specific programming each node receives from a central processing unit.

Conclusion

From the drawbridges of yore to the soaring heights of the Dynamic Tower, kinetic architecture has evolved into a dynamic force in contemporary design. The convergence of aesthetics, functionality, and environmental consciousness positions kinetic architecture at the vanguard of architectural innovation, promising a future where buildings dynamically adapt to our ever-evolving needs.
