Transitioning from Dynamo to Grasshopper and Rhino 7.0: My Journey thus far Pt 1

October 8, 2023 Andres Silva

Embarking on a journey of workflow transformation is never a decision made lightly. It all started when my post-grad requirements came in the mail. I had tried using Grasshopper in the past but to no avail. Coming from a background of Dynamo, I began to realize the potential benefits of exploring other tools and certain limitations between the software packages. This blog post chronicles my transition from Dynamo to Grasshopper and Rhino 7.0, a decision that would ultimately reshape the way I approach the Architecture Industry.

Why I Chose to Make the Switch

The main reason I decided to ultimately switch my entire workflow was the improved functionality and extended reach of additional software packages. Grasshopper and Rhino 7.0 offered a seamless integration of parametric modeling and visual scripting, providing a level of flexibility and precision that Dynamo couldn’t match.

The extensive plugin ecosystem in Rhino allowed me to tap into a broader range of tools and resources, making complex tasks more manageable. Not only that — you can develop micro scripts, save them, and create an ecosystem of scripts that work in tandem, supporting the most complex simulations only available through a Grasshopper and Rhino pathway.

Adapting to Grasshopper

Adapting to Grasshopper was initially a learning curve, but with persistence and the right resources, I began to see significant progress. When I encountered issues I didn’t know how to solve, watching YouTube videos or commenting in community forums always led to a solution.

The way I approached problems learned from Dynamo was largely applicable and transferable. It was essential to approach it systematically, breaking down complex tasks into manageable steps. Through post-grad studies, I learned it was easier to create separate specific scripts that could correlate and create synergies when combined.

You have to admire the way that McNeel and his team of scientists, data analysts, mathematicians, and physicists were so accurate in designing Rhino and Grasshopper as an ecosystem. They act as if linked — something Revit and Dynamo still struggle with.

The Benefits of Using Grasshopper and Rhino 7.0

The transition brought a multitude of benefits. One notable advantage was the graph mappers and attractors functionality — more feature-rich and better executed in Grasshopper than Dynamo. This allowed the development of small scripts that could be added to other scripts and customized to suit the needs of each alteration.

The seamless compatibility between Grasshopper and Rhino allowed for a more fluid design process, resulting in a complete sustainability and environmental analysis run entirely from within Grasshopper.

A special mention has to go out to Group 7 of CPROEN. Together we made this project using Rhino 7.0, Ladybug Tools, Radiance, Open Studio, Energy Plus, and many other plugins to create every part of the design — a testament to what collaborative parametric design can achieve.
