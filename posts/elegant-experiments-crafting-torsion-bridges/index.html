Elegant Experiments: Crafting Torsion Bridges

October 8, 2023 Andres Silva

There’s something inherently beautiful about torsion as a structural phenomenon. Unlike compression or tension — forces that act in a single direction — torsion is a twist, a rotation, a force that gives form its dynamism. When you design with torsion rather than against it, extraordinary things happen.

A Structural Fascination

In the realm of structural design, certain principles captivate beyond engineering necessity. Transmutations, tensegrity, tessellation — and now torsion — represent the frontier where structural logic becomes spatial poetry. This project marks an inaugural foray into the torsional bridge, and the results exceeded expectations while revealing clear paths for refinement.

The 66-Point Attractor System

The technical heart of this design is a 66-point attractor system applied to each cable. Rather than treating cables as static lines between fixed endpoints, these attractors serve as dynamic computational anchors — control points that allow cable geometry to adapt and intricately link with individual spacers throughout each section of the structure.

The result: cables that are not straight but shaped — following attractor logic to create the characteristic torsional twist that defines the bridge’s visual identity. As a first attempt, the system revealed opportunities for refinement in attractor spacing and tension calibration, establishing the geometric foundation from which future iterations will evolve.

A Work in Progress — By Design

The Dynamo code underlying this bridge is explicitly documented as a work in progress. This isn’t a limitation — it’s an honest characterization of how great structural design develops. These bridges, much like the torsional forces they contend with, are in constant evolution: each iteration building upon the previous, refining geometry with increasing precision.

The same script that creates this bridge can, with parameter adjustments, generate an entire family of related torsional forms — each unique, each structurally valid.

Art Meets Engineering

A torsion bridge is not merely a feat of engineering — it’s an eloquent testament to the fusion of art and science. The visible rotation of the structure communicates its structural logic directly to observers, making invisible forces legible in built form.

As the design continues to evolve, this project stands as a compelling starting point: proof that torsion, properly embraced, is among architecture’s most expressive structural vocabularies.
