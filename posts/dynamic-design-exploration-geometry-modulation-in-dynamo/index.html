Dynamic Design Exploration: Geometry Modulation in Dynamo

October 8, 2023 Andres Silva

Every skill requires dedicated practice. This project was exactly that — a focused playground for geometry modulation and manipulation within Dynamo’s visual coding environment. The objective was not a finished building or a deployable structure, but mastery: developing fluency in how parametric systems generate structural form.

The results exceeded expectations.

Two Distinct Variations from One System

The exercise produced two bridge variations, each emerging from the same underlying parametric logic but with one key difference:

Variation 1: Cross-tension wires zigzagging between each other, creating a dynamic X-pattern in the air between structural nodes. From any vantage point, the cables appear to dance — a visual energy that transforms the structure from a simple spanning element into an experience.

Variation 2: Straight cord members traversing end-to-end, gracefully following the contour of the arches. Cleaner, more classical, with the satisfying clarity of geometry that declares exactly what it’s doing.

The evolution between these two designs traces a fascination with mechanically dynamic structural properties — a persistent interest in how geometry and mechanism can coexist in a single structural form.

What the Column Ends Reveal

The most instructive detail in the entire project sits at the column ends. Examine them closely: the columns begin as triangular cross-sections at the base. As they rise toward the apex where they meet the tension arch, they transition through intermediate forms into perfectly rectangular and then diamond cross-sections.

This geometric evolution isn’t arbitrary. Each transition reflects the changing nature of the forces the column must resist at that elevation. Triangular sections provide excellent multi-axis stability near the base; rectangular and diamond sections optimize the connection geometry with the tension cables above. The shape change reads the force diagram and responds.

Code as Clarity

What makes this exercise genuinely valuable is the revelation that structural sophistication doesn’t require complex code. A single, well-structured code produces the entire structure. Each code area serves a distinct purpose. The rotation of the main beam sweeping over the lower bridging deck is legible both in the Dynamo graph and in the physical geometry.

Geometry modulation, done right, is simply clear thinking made visible.
