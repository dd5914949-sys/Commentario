Revisiting the GM-Inspired Tower: Point Attractors, Low-E Glass, and a Parisian Setting

December 20, 2021 Andres Silva

Some projects need to rest before they can be finished. Coming back to this GM-inspired tower with fresh perspective, I was able to complete it properly — and see it in a new light.

Point Attractors: The Core Logic

This project deepened my understanding of point attractors within Revit, working in unison with Dynamo scripts to generate the building’s distinctive facade behavior. Point attractors create zones of influence on the building surface — areas where panels, apertures, or material properties shift in response to their distance from the attractor point.

The challenge: ensuring every other element of the project matched the attractor-driven facade in character and visual creativity. A remarkable parametric facade surrounded by generic conventional design creates cognitive dissonance. The rest of the building needed to earn its place beside the facade.

Finding the Right Setting

The placement decision: the outskirts of Paris, near clusters of contemporary towers, deliberately avoiding the classical arrondissements where this design would clash rather than contribute. This peripheral zone allows the tower to exist on its own terms — drawing attention, inviting admiration, without disrespecting the historical fabric it might otherwise compete with.

In this setting, looking at the finished project, there’s something that architectural images rarely capture: the feeling of completion. I wonder if this is what engineers and architects feel when a project reaches this stage — a recognition that something has been brought into being. “I wonder if that is the same thoughts that engineers and architects go through — are my children ok?”

Low-E Coatings: Quantified Energy Performance

The energy performance story of this tower centers on Low-E (low-emissivity) glass coatings. These coatings absorb or block unwanted thermal energy — infrared radiation — while allowing visible light to pass through. The effect is a facade that lights the interior without heating it.

The technology referenced here, developed and refined by a German manufacturer to the highest standard, quantifies what “performance” actually means:

  • Up to 25% heating and cooling efficiency gains from reduced thermal load through the building envelope
  • Up to 38% reduction in lighting energy requirements met through optimized daylight admission

These aren’t marginal improvements — they represent a fundamental change in the energy budget of a glazed tower. Low-E coatings are one of the most impactful and cost-effective passive performance technologies available to architects specifying high-performance glass facades.
