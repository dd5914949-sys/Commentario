Part V: Solar Optimization — Tinted Glass and Glare Reduction

October 8, 2023 Andres Silva

Building performance doesn’t emerge from good intentions — it emerges from precise calculation. Part V of our tower project represents the payoff of all the preceding parametric work: a rigorous solar study that quantifies how much sun each panel receives, then uses that data to drive a glare reduction strategy that achieves an 82% improvement.

The Solar Study Methodology

Conducting a meaningful solar study on a tessellated facade requires more than simply measuring sunlight exposure. The process demands:

  1. Plane definition — establishing a plane at specific points on each facade panel
  2. Normal vector calculation — computing the vector perpendicular to each panel’s surface
  3. Dot product comparison — comparing each panel’s normal vector against the sun’s direction vector at different times of day

The dot product result is key: when a panel’s normal vector closely aligns with the sun’s direction, the dot product approaches 1.0 — indicating maximum direct solar exposure. When the vectors are perpendicular, the dot product approaches 0 — indicating minimal solar impact.

Color-Mapped Results

The dot product values across all panels are remapped to a visual color scale using a function-like recalibration mechanism:

  • Green signifies optimal lighting without overexposure — ideal conditions for occupant comfort and energy efficiency
  • Red denotes panels susceptible to excessive glare — requiring mitigation strategies

This color-mapped visualization immediately reveals the building’s solar vulnerability: the southern face receives the most intense direct radiation, concentrated in a band that tracks the sun’s path across the facade.

The 82% Glare Reduction Strategy

Armed with precise solar exposure data, we implemented a two-pronged mitigation approach:

Strategy 1 — Voronoi Fragmentation (40% reduction): The tessellated Voronoi pattern applied to each face in Part IV proves its functional value here. By fragmenting the southern glass surface into irregular cells, the direct beam is scattered across multiple surfaces — reducing intensity at any single point by approximately 40%.

Strategy 2 — Light-Reflective Film (additional 42% reduction): A light-reflective film positioned one foot from the top of the southern facade further curtails glare. This secondary layer intercepts light that passes through the fragmented outer surface, producing a combined total glare reduction of 82%.

Architecture as Performance

The tower’s design creates a dynamic juxtaposition between the classical backdrop of its urban context and the tessellated glass facade — a forward-looking statement about what sustainable performance can look like when it’s built directly into the geometric logic of the building skin.
