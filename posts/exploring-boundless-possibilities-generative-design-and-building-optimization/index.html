Exploring Boundless Possibilities: Generative Design and Building Optimization

October 8, 2023 Andres Silva

Generative design represents an iterative creative process driven by programmatic code. It generates a spectrum of outputs that align with predefined constraints set by the designer — who then refines the viable range by selecting specific outputs or tweaking input parameters with each iteration.

This project pushes the boundaries of what that process can produce within the Revit environment, using a Beta generative design integration that brings the full optimization workflow directly into the BIM toolset.

The Design Objective

The optimization brief is precisely stated: minimize cladding surface area while maximizing usable floor space. These two objectives create a productive tension — more floor space typically means more facade, so the algorithm must find configurations where floor area grows more efficiently than cladding grows.

The study encompasses nine input variables, each representing a dimension of the building’s configurable geometry. The Dynamo code underlying this study creates the building from multiple points, generates a diverse array of massing options, and applies the objective function across all variants simultaneously.

Dynamic Site Rotation and Lighting Analysis

Beyond the pure floor-area optimization, the study incorporates two additional analytical layers:

Dynamic site rotation: the building’s orientation is varied to enhance views and sculpt the building’s form in response to its specific urban context.

Lighting analysis: meticulous lighting performance data informs the strategic thinning of glass panels in specific locations, reducing solar insolation where it creates thermal problems while maintaining transparency where occupant experience benefits from it.

Reading the Results

Grey sliders in the output graph filter design variants based on selected criteria. The intersection of the building’s outer walls and the planes representing each floor level — a critical geometric operation — is what produces floors that harmonize with the building’s contours rather than fighting them.

While some algorithmic solutions align with the study’s criteria and required building height, others veer outside the acceptable range. The designer’s role here isn’t to generate manually — it’s to define the criteria, review the outputs, and select the solutions that deserve further development.
