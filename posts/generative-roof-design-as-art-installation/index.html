Generative Roof Design as Art Installation

November 1, 2021 Andres Silva

The roof has always been architecture’s most expressive element. From Le Corbusier’s roof gardens to Utzon’s Sydney Opera House shells, the building top is where design ambition finds its most visible expression. Now parametric tools are giving us the ability to discover roof forms rather than invent them — and the results are extraordinary.

Starting with Points

This project began as a point attractor exercise — deceptively simple in concept, remarkably rich in outcome. Segmented chords are divided into equal parts, with a point placed at each location. A selection process reduces these to specific pairings. Actuation logic then moves each point in the Z-direction — positive or negative — to influence the underlying NURBS curve.

The NURBS curve that emerges is a precise mathematical response to the position and magnitude of each attractor point. Change an attractor, and the entire roof form reorganizes accordingly.

The Layering Breakthrough

The project’s most significant development: adding multiple layers of these arrangements simultaneously, each with different chord lengths and segmented spacing.

This creates a richer solution space for the generative design algorithm to explore. More layers mean more geometric variation, which translates into a dramatically higher number of algorithmically acceptable solutions. By adding surface area filters, acceptable solution counts increased by nearly 233% — with the same computational sample size.

The algorithm simply had more valid geometry to work with. Geometry produced more solutions. Better solutions produced better architecture.

The Hadid Moment

Among the thousands of solutions generated, one stopped the exploration cold: a form bearing unmistakable resemblance to Zaha Hadid’s Heydar Aliyev Centre in Baku, Azerbaijan — one of contemporary architecture’s most celebrated fluid forms.

The generative algorithm, working entirely from point-attractor logic, had independently arrived at a geometry that one of the world’s greatest architects spent years developing.

This is the profound implication of generative design: great forms have structural and mathematical logic underlying their beauty. When your algorithm is correctly set up, it finds the same geometry that great designers intuited by hand. The tools don’t replace creativity — they reveal the mathematics hiding inside it.
