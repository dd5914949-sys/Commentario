Part I: Tessellated, Optimized & Light-Analysed — The Tower Project

June 30, 2021 Andres Silva

What if a building’s exterior could literally design itself around performance? That’s exactly the question at the heart of this tower project — and Dynamo’s generative design engine gave us the tools to find out.

The Generative Design Approach

At Verdi Studio, parametric tools aren’t just drafting shortcuts — they’re intelligence engines. For this tower, we built a Dynamo script controlled by 9 distinct input variables, each governing a different aspect of the building’s geometry: tower height and floor count, facade rotation per floor, cladding panel dimensions, setback distances, window-to-wall ratios, and structural grid spacing.

By feeding these variables into Dynamo’s generative design solver, we evaluated thousands of geometric combinations simultaneously and filtered for solutions that minimized total cladding surface area while maintaining optimal daylight penetration.

Why Minimize Cladding?

Facade systems are among the most resource-intensive components of any building. By algorithmically reducing cladding area, we directly reduce material consumption, embodied carbon, and long-term maintenance costs — without compromising form or performance.

The results were striking: the algorithm identified tower configurations that reduced facade surface by up to 23% compared to a conventional prismatic form, while maintaining equivalent floor plate areas.

Light Analysis Integration

Using Dynamo’s integration with Revit’s analysis tools, each generated solution was evaluated for solar exposure. We prioritized geometries that diffused direct sunlight across floors, reducing glare and cooling loads — a critical consideration for towers in high-sun environments.

The Power of Code-Driven Design

Every element of this study was generated parametrically. No manual geometry was drawn. This means design changes — a taller tower, a different site, a new program requirement — propagate instantly through the entire model.

This is Part I of a multi-part series exploring generative design across every scale of the project: from mass study to site placement, facade tessellation to solar insolation analysis.
