Offshore Solar Re-Cycle: Wind, Sun, and Repurposed Plastic on the Water

June 13, 2021 Andres Silva

Energy transition isn’t just about replacing fossil fuels with clean alternatives — it’s about rethinking the entire architecture of how we generate power. This project proposes an integrated offshore system where wind and solar become a single unified structure, and where the very material used to build the platform is reclaimed from the waste stream.

Combined Efficiencies: The Core Idea

The foundation of this design is a simple but powerful observation: a wind turbine occupies significant volume in the wind-rich offshore environment, but captures energy only through its blades. The mast, nacelle housing, and substructure are all structural — they don’t generate power. What if they could?

This solution wraps the entire post of the wind generator with modular solar panels — from below the blade attachment point down to the water line. The same PV wrap technology is extended to the generator housing and its side panels. What was inert structural volume becomes an additional energy-generating surface. The wind turbine’s own geometry becomes the mounting structure for a solar array.

Recycling’s Potential: The Barge Platform

The solar arrays mounted on the floating barge platform are modular — they can be assembled, expanded, and relocated. The barge itself is the innovation: fabricated entirely from repurposed plastic materials through a three-step circular process:

  1. Pay for plastic bottle collection and recycling, including lids
  2. Melt waste and mixed plastics into 3D-printable feedstock
  3. Print barge structure and solar panel mounting frames from recycled material

The impact is measurable: approximately 40 kg of plastic waste eliminated per 36 KW/hr installation. The wind turbine’s base incorporates 65% recycled plastic and smelting slag — including 10% construction waste converted to aggregate-sized components — reducing the demand for virgin sand and aggregate stone by over 65% per platform.

A Design Born from Passion

This project sits at the intersection of everything that drives this work: computational design skill applied to problems that genuinely matter. The passion for sustainable design that motivates every project finds its clearest expression here — not just using sustainable materials, but designing systems where waste is the input and clean energy is the output.

This is part of a continuing future energy solutions initiative. The global energy predicament isn’t going to be solved by one technology — it requires reimagining the relationship between existing infrastructure, new materials, and digital design capability.
