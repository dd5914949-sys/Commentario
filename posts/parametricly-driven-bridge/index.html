Parametrically Driven Bridge Over the Seine

June 21, 2021 Andres Silva

Every practice has a project that changes the way it thinks. For us, this bridge was that project — the moment when parametric tools stopped being scripting aids and became structural design engines.

The Structural Ambition

The ambition was clear: design an entire functioning bridge structure using only Dynamo, with no manual geometry. Every component — deck, arch, cables, foundations, structural members — would emerge from code.

The goal wasn’t just efficiency. It was to test whether parametric tools could handle the full complexity of civil-scale structural design. The answer, definitively, was yes.

Learning from the Master

Santiago Calatrava has long been an architectural and engineering inspiration — an extraordinary figure who refuses to separate structural necessity from visual poetry. The Margaret Hunt Hill Bridge in Dallas exemplifies this: a single inclined arch that handles gravity, wind, and dynamic loads while producing a landmark visible from miles away.

A similar bridge encountered near a school in Burnaby, B.C. reinforced this admiration. From that moment, the relationship between structural forces and architectural expression became a central preoccupation of this practice. This bridge is that preoccupation made computational.

All Components in Dynamo

Every structural element was modeled in Dynamo using code, then exported to Revit as either instances or structural members depending on their category. This process was critical for developing deep fluency in Dynamo data management — understanding how lists of elements, indices, and geometric references flow through a parametric graph to produce coherent structure.

The Structural Solution

The raised curve over the Seine is achieved through bilateral tension cable arrays — multiple cables on each side, precisely calibrated to maintain tension distribution and structural rigidity across the full span. This minimizes ground supports required while allowing the distinctive arch profile to carry deck loads efficiently.

The Commitment to Code

From this project forward: modeling entirely in code. Not because it’s faster initially (often it isn’t), but because code-based models are legible, auditable, and adaptable in ways that manual models never are. Change a parameter, and the entire structure updates. That’s not just convenience — it’s a fundamentally different relationship between designer and design.
