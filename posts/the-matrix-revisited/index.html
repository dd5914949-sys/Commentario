The Matrix Revisited: Navigating Structural Iterations in Real Time

September 14, 2021 Andres Silva

Last week’s post introduced a parametrically generated tensioned cable structure. This week, the design space becomes navigable — with both walkthrough and flyby animations that let you move through multiple structural iterations simultaneously within a single model.

Seeing All Variants at Once

What makes this project genuinely powerful isn’t any single design — it’s the ability to see all the designs at once. The flyby animation sweeps across the diverse structural iterations that coexist within the parametric model, making the relationship between design variables and structural form directly visible.

In the walkthrough view, you can clearly appreciate the alternating structures, how each responds to the tilting and curving of the bridge deck pieces, and how individual endpoints are connected by both rods and tensioning cables at each section and subsection of the structure.

Why This Changes Project Management

The capacity to navigate design changes throughout a live structure isn’t just a visualization technique — it fundamentally transforms the project management conversation.

Rapid solution iteration provides rich, immediate feedback. Clients and stakeholders who might struggle to interpret static drawings or conventional renders find navigable parametric environments intuitive: they can explore, compare, and respond. The number of viable design options they can meaningfully engage with expands dramatically.

The result: better-informed decisions, faster alignment, and designs that more precisely reflect what the client actually needs — not what they could approximately communicate through conventional methods.

Augmented Reality on the Horizon

The 3D-baked material version of this model, prepared for Augmented Reality delivery, would transform the experience entirely. Imagine placing this structure at full scale, in a real location, and walking through it wearing an AR headset — with the ability to cycle through design variants in real space, in real time.

For anyone with access to AR hardware: this is the near-term future of architectural design review. The gap between the digital model and the physical experience is closing rapidly, and projects designed parametrically — like this one — are ideally positioned to make the transition.
