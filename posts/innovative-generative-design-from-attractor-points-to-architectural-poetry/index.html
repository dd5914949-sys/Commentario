Innovative Generative Design: From Attractor Points to Architectural Poetry

October 8, 2023 Andres Silva

The best design explorations begin with a modest premise and end somewhere extraordinary. This project began as a simple point attractor exercise — dividing segmented chords into precise fractions — and evolved into one of the most generatively rich design processes attempted.

From Points to Architecture

The placement of points along chord segments marked the beginning of a selection process, empowering the freedom to curate chosen pairings. Once pairings were established, a transformative actuation in the Z-direction occurred — each selected point moving upward or downward in response to its attractor relationship, orchestrating a flowing, wavelike movement along the underlying NURBS curve.

The movement is not random. It follows a mathematical rule derived from the attractor geometry — meaning the form that emerges is not designed in the conventional sense, but generated through principled logic. The NURBS curve provides continuity and smoothness; the attractor points provide local variation. The combination produces form that is simultaneously ordered and organic.

Unveiling Layers of Complexity

The pivotal breakthrough came with the integration of multiple layers, each characterized by distinct lengths and segmented intervals. This addition transformed what had been a relatively constrained exploration into a genuinely rich design space.

Early stages struggled with solutions that harmonized with both Revit and Dynamo simultaneously — a common challenge when generative algorithms produce geometry that looks compelling but fails to translate cleanly into the BIM environment. Through strategic filtering and meticulous command application, a marked improvement of nearly 233% in acceptable solutions was achieved. More valid geometry, less iteration waste.

The Hadid Resonance

During the selection process — an artful examination of patterns, isolating solutions with the desired aesthetic through the visual GUI — one variant emerged that bore a striking resemblance to Zaha Hadid’s Heydar Aliyev Centre in Baku, Azerbaijan. The fluid, continuous surface quality of that building, achieved through years of specialized architectural development, appeared here as one output among many in a generative dataset.

This isn’t coincidence — it’s validation. The most expressive contemporary architectural forms are often the ones that generative systems naturally produce when freed from conventional geometric constraints. Zaha Hadid’s work and computational design share a common mathematical language.
