Rail Station: A Story Over 7 Years in the Making

November 7, 2021 Andres Silva

To tell this story correctly, you have to go back to the moment I was finishing high school credits and preparing to apply to university. Design was already the obsession — and this rail station began as one of my first experimental designs, before I knew it would take seven years to reach its full realization.

The Original Vision: The Roof as Ocean

The concept was immediately clear: a roof structure that moved like the sea. Fluid, free-flowing, with heights and contours that undulated in all directions simultaneously — not the disciplined curves of classical architecture, but the organic complexity of a wave caught mid-break.

The approach: create a mass from various slices of contours, shaping the form through layered sections, then place walls that follow the geometry precisely. The mass I created through hard work, dedication, and substantial trial and error became one of those rare design outcomes where the final result exceeds the original ambition.

Those UV Issues — Years of Them

For years, the structural challenge blocked progress. The UV mapping problem was persistent: sections cut through the curved mass kept altering themselves along the Y-plane, deforming in ways that made structural coordination impossible.

Multiple approaches were tested: three-deck configurations, flat I-beam framing, manual truss modeling for each individual section. Each approach revealed a new constraint introduced by the fluid roof geometry. Without a tool that could generate structure from mathematical coordinates rather than manual drafting, the design was unreachable.

The breakthrough came when Dynamo entered the toolkit. Suddenly, the mathematical understanding of what the structure needed — already present — had a path to physical expression.

Sustaina-Pro Concrete: Material Innovation

While the structural challenges were being resolved, a parallel investigation developed a novel concrete specification for the project. Sustaina-Pro Concrete incorporates industrial by-products into the aggregate matrix:

  • Metal slag from steel production
  • Fly ash from coal-fired power generation
  • Crushed rubber as a proportion of aggregate

These additions create a compression performance reduction that is addressed through a higher cement-to-aggregate-to-water ratio — maintaining structural performance while incorporating industrial and post-consumer waste streams that would otherwise require disposal.

What Comes Next

The following post will address the different techniques used to express how the underlying architecture flows with traffic routes — above in the skyways and shops, below in the corridor system — using a construction simulation and a 5D BIM presentation with cost overlays.

5D means cost. 4D means time. And the 3D is the structure — all of it generated in Dynamo. Seven years after the first sketch, this project is finally speaking every dimension simultaneously.
