Transitioning from Dynamo to Grasshopper and Rhino 7.0: Energy Analysis and Environmental Studies

October 8, 2023 Andres Silva

Accuracy and Functionality Comparison

Dynamo and Grasshopper are both powerful visual programming tools, but they serve distinct purposes within the realm of computational design. Grasshopper, particularly when integrated with Rhino 7.0, excels in complex geometric modeling and environmental simulations due to its robust mathematical engine and extensive plugin ecosystem.

Grasshopper’s precision is renowned in the design community. It operates on a high-fidelity numerical representation of geometry, making it an ideal platform for projects that demand intricate geometries and precise environmental analyses. While Dynamo is a valuable tool for parametric design within Revit, it lacks the mathematical rigor and robust geometry engine found in Grasshopper.

Integration and Interoperability

One of the standout advantages of Grasshopper is its exceptional ability to interface with a wide array of software: finite element analysis tools like ANSYS, environmental simulation tools like Ladybug Tools, and data analysis platforms like Excel and Python.

Dynamo, primarily designed for the Autodesk ecosystem, is inherently limited in its interoperability outside Autodesk — a significant drawback for professionals who require seamless integration with a wider range of software applications.

Energy Analysis in Grasshopper

Rhino with Grasshopper stands out for energy analysis due to its integration with Ladybug Tools, which provides a comprehensive suite for solar radiation, daylighting, and energy modeling. The integration of Radiance further enhances its capabilities for daylight analysis — the level of detail from daylighting analysis results is stunning compared to other software.

Optimization Techniques for Sustainable Design

Grasshopper’s extensive plugin ecosystem includes powerful optimization tools like Galapagos, Octopus, and Millipede, which enable designers to explore vast design spaces and discover optimal solutions based on multiple criteria including energy performance.

In one project, a multi-optimizing solution was used for a generative design approach — focusing on aesthetics and occupancy comfort while validating sustainable criteria. Peak energy intensity was brought down to 0.76 kWh/m², passing Netherlands standards, through the use of a thermal heat-pump and sensors on windows and every floor to control passive cooling and heating.

Conclusion

Rhino with Grasshopper emerges as the superior choice for energy analysis and environmental studies. Its accuracy in simulations, wide-ranging interoperability, and powerful optimization capabilities set it apart from Dynamo.
