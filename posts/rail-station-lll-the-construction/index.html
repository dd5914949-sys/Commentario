Rail Station III: The Construction — BIM-Driven Project Management at Scale

November 22, 2021 Andres Silva

Design is only as good as its execution. A stunning parametric model means nothing if it can’t survive contact with a real construction site. In Part III of our rail station series, we turn from geometry to delivery — and discover how BIM coordination fundamentally transforms project outcomes.

The Construction Challenge

Rail station construction is among the most complex in the built environment. You’re coordinating structural steel, mechanical systems, electrical infrastructure, and passenger flow — often simultaneously, on a constrained urban site with live adjacent systems still operating.

Our approach: let BIM do the heavy lifting before a single piece of steel is erected.

Clash Detection as Pre-emptive Risk Management

Using Revit’s coordination model, we ran systematic clash detection across all MEP and structural disciplines before construction began. The results were eye-opening: over 340 potential clashes identified, each one a conflict that — if discovered on site — would translate to costly delays and change orders.

By resolving these in the model, we eliminated the vast majority of field RFIs before they could occur. This isn’t just time-saving — it fundamentally changes the risk profile of a project.

The Underground Passage

Perhaps the most technically demanding element of this project was the basement pedestrian passage — a below-grade connection linking the station to adjacent transit infrastructure. This involved careful coordination of:

  • Structural sequencing to prevent differential settlement
  • Waterproofing continuity between poured sections
  • Mechanical routing in extremely tight vertical clearances
  • Phased excavation to maintain structural stability of adjacent infrastructure

Dynamo scripts automated the sequencing analysis, generating time-linked 4D models that let us visualize the build sequence and identify potential conflicts before they materialized on site.

Scheduling as Design Discipline

One of the most powerful insights from this project: construction scheduling isn’t just an administrative task — it’s a design discipline. How you sequence a build determines what’s possible, what’s risky, and what gets compromised. By integrating our BIM model directly with scheduling software, we could test sequencing options parametrically and optimize for minimum critical-path duration.

The earlier BIM discipline is applied, the greater the return on coordination investment.
